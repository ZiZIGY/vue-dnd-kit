## What to look at

- **`Square.vue`**: each of the 64 squares is a drop target whose `accept` is a function. It is
  asked while a piece is dragged, so the square takes that piece's legal moves only; a drop
  anywhere else is refused and the piece flies back. The legal squares light up from
  `isAccepting`: no state of the game's own tracks the drag. The square the piece came from reads
  `useDnD().active`.
- **`Piece.vue`**: a plain draggable, `disabled` when it isn't its side's turn. `keyboard:
  'droppables'` makes the arrow keys jump between the squares that accept the piece: its legal
  moves, nothing else. `snapCenterToCursor` keeps the piece centered under the pointer, and a touch
  drags at once (`activation`) since a piece doesn't scroll the page.
- **The overlay** is the default clone of the piece, styled from `[data-dnd-overlay]`: it lifts
  (larger, a long shadow) while `'dragging'` and settles as it lands, `'dropping'`. The piece left
  on the square fades (`isDragging`).
- **`index.vue`**: `announcements` speak pieces and squares — "Picked up the white knight on g1",
  "f3" — instead of ids.
- **`chess.ts`** and **`game.ts`**: the rules and the game, with no drag and drop in them: legal
  moves with check, castling, en passant and promotion, the notation, undo. A move in `onDrop` makes
  a new position; the pieces re-render on their new squares, and the overlay lands on the moved one.
